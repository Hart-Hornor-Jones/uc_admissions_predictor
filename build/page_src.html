Predicting UC admission rates from public school data

Every dot below is a California public high school. Its height is the school's actual admission rate to one University of California campus (admitted applicants as a share of all its applicants, three entering classes pooled); its horizontal position is the rate predicted by a statistical model built from nothing but public statistics about the school — no application files. You choose the model's ingredients on the left; the model refits instantly. If the dots hug the dashed diagonal, the campus's decisions are largely reconstructible from those school statistics; if they scatter, they are not. The two charts share your ingredient list but can show different campuses or eras, side by side. Click any dot for the school behind it and the arithmetic of its prediction. Scroll or pinch on a chart to zoom; double-click (or double-tap) to reset.

Definitions, method, and how to read the numbers
Eras.
“2016–19 (test era)”: entering classes when SAT/ACT scores were required. “2023–25 (test-blind)”: entering classes after the University of California stopped considering admission test scores. The transition years 2020–22 are excluded (a pandemic-disrupted admissions cycle, and state testing gaps).
The fit.
Weighted least squares — the standard technique that chooses the line (here, a weighted sum of your selected variables plus a base rate) minimizing the squared prediction errors. Each school is weighted by its number of applicants, so high-volume schools count for more and noisy small-school rates count for less. Each variable is standardized first (converted to a z-score: how many standard deviations the school sits above or below the applicant-weighted average school), so coefficients are comparable across variables.
R².
The share of school-to-school variation in admission rates that the model reproduces, from 0 (no better than guessing the average for everyone) to 1 (exact). “Adjusted R²” applies a small penalty for the number of variables used. “Typical miss” is the root-mean-square prediction error, in percentage points.
Coefficients.
Percentage points of predicted admission rate per one standard deviation of the variable, holding the other selected variables fixed; the parenthesized number is the standard error, a measure of statistical uncertainty. When two selected variables are nearly duplicates, their individual coefficients are not meaningful even though the prediction is — see the caution under the table.
Samples.
Schools with at least 30 pooled applicants to the campus. The University masks small counts (fewer than 5 applicants or 3 admitted) in its published data; rates here are computed only from visible years, and a school is dropped when most of its applicant volume is masked. A school also drops from a fit if it is missing one of your selected variables — the “n” beside each chart updates.
Variables.
CAASPP: California’s statewide grade-11 tests (share meeting the standard, from each class’s junior-year spring). A–G completion: share of a school’s graduating class finishing the course sequence required for University of California and California State University eligibility. UPP (“unduplicated pupil percentage”): the state’s operational poverty measure — the share of students who are low-income (free/reduced-price-meal eligible), English learners, or foster youth. SED: share of grade-11 students classified socioeconomically disadvantaged. EL: share classified English learners. LCFF+ flag: whether UPP is at least 75%, the statutory line above which a school draws concentration funding under California’s Local Control Funding Formula — included so you can test whether crossing the official line matters beyond poverty itself. School racial composition is from state enrollment counts (grades 9–12; “Asian” includes the separate Filipino category). Neighborhood variables describe the census tract around the school (American Community Survey 5-year estimates). Applicant-pool GPA: the mean UC-recalculated grade point average of the school’s applicants, as published by the University. Application rate: applicants per 100 course-eligible seniors. Past 6-year graduation: how the school’s earlier students (entering 2004–10) fared after enrolling anywhere in the UC system. Past yield: of the school’s applicants admitted to this campus in the five entry years preceding the era, the share who chose to enroll — a way to test whether a school’s enrollment history feeds back into its admission odds. Distance to campus: the straight-line distance from the school to the selected campus, on a logarithmic scale (1.0 = 10 km, 2.0 = 100 km, 3.0 = 1,000 km), from state school-location records. Distance turns out to matter a great deal at some campuses and not at all at others — try “Distance only” with San Diego in the test era. Charter school: 1 if the school is a charter in state 2023–24 records, 0 if district-run; the same flag is applied to both eras (charter status rarely changes, but conversions exist). Pool-GPA inflation: how much the school’s applicant-pool GPA rose from the test era to the test-blind era beyond what the change in its own test results (CAASPP) predicts, in GPA points — a school-level grade-inflation measure. It is computed across the full 2016–2025 window, so in the test era read it as “schools that went on to inflate.” It is available for the 969 highest-volume schools; selecting it restricts the sample to them.
Model.
“Linear” is the weighted least squares fit described above (the default). “Logistic” fits the log-odds of admission by iteratively reweighted least squares, a quasi-binomial fit in which each school’s admitted count is its applicants times its admission rate, so predictions stay between 0 and 100%. Its coefficients are log-odds per standard deviation (near a 25% admission rate, 0.1 in log-odds is about 2 percentage points), and its standard errors allow for school-level variation beyond binomial chance. R² and typical miss are computed from the fitted rates in percentage points for both models.
Fit model to.
“All schools” (the default) estimates the model on every school in the chart. “Schools outside the campus’s county” estimates it without the local schools and then applies it to them, so their actual-minus-predicted values are out-of-sample comparisons with similar schools elsewhere in California. Local schools are those in the county of the chart’s campus (Alameda for Berkeley, Yolo for Davis, Orange for Irvine, and so on; San Diego and Imperial counties for San Diego). The “Local school” indicator can be added to the model only when it is fitted to all schools; it is left as 0 or 1 rather than standardized, so its coefficient is the difference between local and other schools, the other selected variables held fixed.
View.
“Actual against predicted” is the chart described above. “Actual minus predicted, by distance” puts each school’s actual-minus-predicted rate on the vertical axis against its distance to the campus on the horizontal axis (log scale); the dashed line is zero difference.
Actual minus predicted, by group.
For a group of schools: the group’s admitted applicants divided by its applicants, minus the applicant-weighted average of the predicted rates. “Admits above prediction” is that difference times the group’s applicants. The standard error combines school-level scatter around the model (estimated from the fitting sample) and uncertainty in the fitted coefficients.
Added variables.
GPA curvature adds squared and cubed terms of the applicant-pool GPA (select it with the GPA itself). Applicant GPA relative to the other campuses: the school’s applicant-pool GPA at this campus minus the applicant-weighted mean of its applicant-pool GPAs at the other eight campuses, same era (the University’s all-campus applicant pool is not in this page’s data, so this stands in for “relative to all UC applicants”). Campus share of the school’s applications: the school’s applicants to this campus as a share of its applicants to all nine campuses, same era (applications, not unique applicants). Admission rate at the other campuses: the school’s admitted applicants divided by its applicants at the other eight campuses, same era. These three use only the campus–era cells in which the school has at least 30 pooled applicants, so campuses where it sent fewer are left out of the sums. LCFF+ flag (UPP above 75%): the flag under the corrected rule, computed from the UPP value shown (rounded to one decimal); it differs from the original flag only for school-eras whose UPP rounds to exactly 75.0. Local school: 1 if the school is in the campus’s county (see “Fit model to”). School counties are from the state public school directory.

Model variables

Grey number = gain in R² if added to the left chart’s model.

Fitted coefficients — percentage points of admission rate per one standard deviation

Caution: when selected variables are strongly correlated (for example, UPP and SED measure nearly the same thing, correlating at about 0.99), the fit can split one real effect between them in arbitrary ways — even with opposite signs — without changing the predictions. Judge a variable by what it adds to R², not by its coefficient in a crowded model.

Sources. Admissions counts and applicant grade point averages: University of California Information Center, “Admissions by source school” (California public high schools; freshman applicants). School test results and grade-11 group enrollment: California Department of Education, CAASPP research files. Course-sequence completion: California Department of Education A–G reports. Student poverty (UPP): California Department of Education, cumulative enrollment / LCFF data. School racial composition: California Department of Education enrollment by race/ethnicity, grades 9–12. Neighborhood measures: U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey 5-year estimates for the school’s census tract. Past graduation and enrollment outcomes: University of California undergraduate outcomes by source school. School locations and charter status: California Department of Education public school directory files. All data are public; schools are identified by their College Board (CEEB) school codes.
